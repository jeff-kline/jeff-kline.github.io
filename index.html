New Hadamard orders above 104

New Hadamard matrices are constructed at six orders: 159052 = 4·17·2339, 824332 = 4·206083 and 22425772 = 4·31·223·811, and 25072 = 16·1567, 46192 = 16·2887 and 47968 = 32·1499. The first three come from a theorem: a quaternary conference matrix of order n + 1 with n odd, and a prime q = 2nr − 1, give a Hadamard matrix of order 4(2nr + 1). Turyn’s power construction raises conference matrices of orders 142 and 322, published by Zaitsev and by Kumar, to higher powers, and Kumar’s 2026 quaternary form of Yamada’s construction completes the matrix. These three orders lie beyond every printed table, and none of the known constructions checked reaches them. The last three come from two dihedral Golay pairs placed on commuting tensor factors, which give order 16(m + n). Each halves the smallest order recorded for its odd part; the orders 4·1567, 4·2887 and 4·1499 remain open.

Rigid woven Hadamard matrices

Meisner’s square builds a Hadamard matrix of order m2 from one of order m using rank‑one sign blocks. In the square and in a new four-group construction, as the output order N grows, almost every labelling gives a matrix whose entries reveal its row and column groups and whose only nonidentity signed symmetry negates all rows and columns together. For each fixed input, the number C of rigid classes, up to signed permutations and transposition, satisfies log C = N log N − cN + O(√N log2 N), where c = 2 − log 2 for the square and 2 + (log 2)⁠/⁠8 for four groups. At order 1600, one design and one order-20 matrix give more than 101514 rigid regular classes. A binary-rank bound shows that if every row and column group in a rank‑one tiling has size at least four, then 16 | N.

Vertex‑transitive Hadamard tournaments

Hadamard tournaments correspond to skew Hadamard matrices. In 1984 Ito showed that a vertex-transitive one has order pt2 for a prime p, and raised the possibility that the order is always a power of p. The paper proves that any exception needs a large fourth-power divisor: for each bound, only finitely many non-prime-power orders have largest fourth-power divisor below it. When no fourth power divides the order, the order is p or p3 and the tournament comes from a difference set in a p-group. The paper also settles every order below 105: such a tournament exists exactly when the order is a prime power congruent to 3 modulo 4.

Modular Hadamard matrices at large orders

Square matrices with entries ±1 and rows pairwise orthogonal modulo m exist at every order divisible by 4 for m = 7, 11, and 13. The case m = 7 answers Problem 11 in a 2026 survey of Hadamard matrices. The paper also proves existence at all sufficiently large orders divisible by 4 for every prime modulus, every power of 2, and composite moduli such as 140. For any fixed modulus m, it proves existence at all sufficiently large orders divisible by a power of 2 that depends on m. Only the abstract of Eliahou’s closely related 2026 paper was openly accessible; the comparison with it rests on that abstract.

A symmetric Hadamard matrix of order 8740

This closes an order stated as open in the 2025 census of Đoković and Seberry: a symmetric Hadamard matrix of order 8740 = 4·2185. It comes from a symmetric version of Seberry’s 1980 recursion: whenever q ≡ 3 (mod 4) is a prime power and a symmetric Hadamard matrix of order q + 5 exists, there are symmetric Hadamard matrices of orders (q + 1)⁠(q + 4)⁠qr for every r. Seberry had these orders for ordinary matrices, and Kumar the symmetric case r = 0; 8740 is the case q = 19, r = 1.

Skew Hadamard matrices of orders 4040, 5192, 5960 and 6216

Four new orders of skew Hadamard matrices: 4040, 5192, 5960 and 6216. Each is 23 times an odd part, 505, 649, 745 or 777, for which the smallest known skew order so far needed a higher power of two, as high as 211 for 505. The matrices come from prime fields with p ≡ 17 (mod 32), and one ratio of binomial coefficients decides which primes work for four fixed patterns. A sequel to order 2120.

Automorphisms of the order‑18 Jacobi Hadamard matrices

Earlier work built skew Hadamard matrices of orders 4(343d + 1), d odd, from order‑18 cyclotomic patterns in the Wallis–⁠Whiteman array, but left open whether they are new up to equivalence. They are, for every comparison made here: none is equivalent to a Kronecker product, a skew doubling, its own transpose, or a matrix built from sixth-power classes. Up to an overall sign, every symmetry fixes the border and applies one map x ↦ ax343j, a an eighteenth power, to all eight field copies; there are 19 at order 1,376. A second pattern set extends the construction to every prime p ≡ 7 or 31 (mod 36), giving at least eight inequivalent matrices per order and sixteen at order 1,376.

Counting intervals, counting eigenvalues

Integrating a function over each interval of a nested subdivision of [0,1] and adding the result back on that interval defines a linear operator with one term per interval, whose only nonzero eigenvalue is that interval’s length. Earlier work on a sparse Mertens kernel showed that the eigenvalues above t track the intervals longer than t when each piece becomes a vanishing fraction of the interval it was cut from, and asked whether that condition can be dropped. Because the terms overlap, it cannot: this paper shows that the ratio of the two counts can oscillate forever even when that fraction is merely kept below a fixed bound, however small, and proves other conditions under which the counts agree, including for the intervals defined by continued-fraction digits.
